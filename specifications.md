Research Project
The group research project is worth 25% of the final grade and is due at 11:59 pm on Friday 9 October 2026. Each project must be completed in a group of exactly two students, unless otherwise approved by the unit coordinator.

A central part of CITS4403 is the computational model your group will design, implement, explore, and analyse. The project will be assessed through the report, submitted code, and project demonstration. These components should complement one another: the report should explain the investigation, the code should reproduce the reported results, and the demonstration should present the model and key findings.

This assessment evaluates your ability to:

formulate a focused research question about a complex system;
select and justify an appropriate modelling framework;
translate modelling assumptions into a computational implementation;
design systematic experiments;
analyse model behaviour using qualitative and quantitative evidence;
interpret results critically and identify limitations; and
communicate and collaborate effectively.
Project Structure
The choice of complex system is open-ended, but your project should demonstrate a clear connection to the concepts, behaviours, and computational modelling approaches covered in the unit.

Building a simulation alone is not sufficient. Your model must be used to investigate a clearly defined research question or hypothesis.

For example, you might examine how system behaviour changes with different parameters, interaction rules, network structures, interventions, or modelling assumptions.

A complete project should include:

a clearly defined system and motivation;
a focused research question or hypothesis;
a computational model with explicit assumptions and rules;
a justified experimental design;
qualitative and quantitative analysis;
interpretation of the results; and
discussion of limitations and possible extensions.
This is a fourth-year research project, and stronger projects will go beyond simply implementing an existing model. They should demonstrate depth in implementation and analysis, and include an element of independent investigation, such as exploring a new research question, extending an existing model, comparing alternative modelling assumptions, or investigating behaviour that has not been examined in the same way.

Start with a simple model, build your understanding of the system, and introduce additional complexity where it is justified. Use the lab sessions to discuss your ideas, modelling decisions, experiments, and results with the facilitators.
